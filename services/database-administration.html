Database Administration

Boring, on purpose.

Performance tuning, replication, backup strategy and — the part most teams skip — recovery you have actually rehearsed, so the plan works on the day you need it.

Start a project How we work

Overview

A backup you have never restored is not a backup. It is a file, and an assumption. The gap between the two only becomes visible at the worst possible moment, which is why we rehearse restores rather than merely scheduling dumps.

The rest of the work is quieter still: finding the queries that are slow because of a missing index, spotting the table that has grown past what its design anticipated, setting up replication so a failure means a failover rather than an outage.

This discipline succeeds by being invisible. Nobody congratulates a database that simply worked all quarter. That is the goal.

The problem

What usually brings people here.

If more than one of these sounds familiar, this is the right page.

Where it hurts
  • The application has slowed noticeably as data volume grew, with no clear cause identified.
  • Backups are scheduled, but nobody has ever performed a full restore to check they work.
  • One database server is a single point of failure for the whole business.
  • Queries were written years ago and nobody has reviewed them against current data volumes.
What we do about it
  • Query analysis that identifies the specific statements consuming your resources.
  • A recovery plan that has been rehearsed end to end, with the recovery time measured.
  • Replication and failover so a server loss becomes a degradation, not an outage.
  • Index and configuration review sized against your current and projected data volumes.

Process

How the engagement runs.

Each stage produces something you can hold, review and act on — so you always know where the work stands.

Health check

Configuration, index usage, slow query log, growth trends, backup status. Most estates have two or three findings that account for the majority of the pain.

Output: Written findings, prioritised

Fix the expensive things

The slowest queries and the missing indexes first, measured before and after so the improvement is demonstrable.

Output: Measured performance improvement

Prove recovery

Restore into an isolated environment and time it. This is the step that most often surprises people.

Output: Verified recovery time

Add resilience

Replication, automated failover and monitoring appropriate to how much downtime your business can absorb.

Output: Replication and failover configured

Ongoing watch

Growth trends, query regressions and capacity headroom reviewed on a schedule.

Output: Monitoring and periodic review
