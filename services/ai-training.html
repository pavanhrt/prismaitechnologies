AI Training

Enablement, not dependency.

Hands-on training for corporate teams and colleges, built around your own tools and data rather than generic exercises — so people can apply it on Monday instead of forgetting it by Friday.

Start a project How we work

Overview

Most AI training is a demo. Someone shows a chat window, everybody nods, and nothing changes in how the team works because nobody practised on anything real.

We build sessions around your actual workflows. If your team processes claims, the exercises use claim documents. If they write reports, they practise on your report formats. The transfer problem — knowing something in a workshop but not applying it at your desk — is the whole difficulty, and generic material makes it worse.

For colleges the goal is different: employability. Students need the tooling that appears in job descriptions and the practice to speak about it credibly in interviews. We teach the fundamentals honestly, including the limitations, because graduates who over-claim get found out quickly.

The problem

What usually brings people here.

If more than one of these sounds familiar, this is the right page.

Where it hurts
  • The team has AI tool access but no shared sense of what to use it for.
  • Previous training was generic, so nothing carried over into daily work.
  • Staff use AI informally with no guidance on what data is safe to paste into it.
  • Graduates finish courses without the practical tooling employers actually ask for.
What we do about it
  • Sessions built around your workflows, with exercises drawn from your real material.
  • Clear guidance on data handling: what can go into which tool, and what cannot.
  • Practice under supervision, so mistakes happen in the session rather than in production.
  • Follow-up after the workshop, when the real questions actually surface.

Process

How the engagement runs.

Each stage produces something you can hold, review and act on — so you always know where the work stands.

Scope with the team

We talk to the people attending, not only whoever is booking. The gap between the two is where irrelevant training comes from.

Output: Agreed syllabus and objectives

Build on your material

Exercises are constructed from your documents, your systems, your formats — de-identified where required.

Output: Custom exercise set

Teach hands-on

Short explanation, then practice, then review. People spend most of the session doing rather than watching.

Output: Delivered sessions

Assess honestly

A practical task at the end that shows who can now do the thing, so you know where to reinforce.

Output: Assessment results

Follow up

A session two to four weeks later, once people have hit real problems. This is where most of the durable learning happens.

Output: Follow-up clinic
